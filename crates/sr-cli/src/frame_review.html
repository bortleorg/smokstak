What these measurements can and cannot prove
  • Frame weight is the factor before local weighting. It is not effective exposure, effective photon count or the final per-pixel contribution.
  • Guide rejection fraction measures suppressed guide locations. It is not the fraction of discarded exposures, and it is not an exact rejected-pixel count in the output master.
  • Native crops preserve the stored sensor samples. Registered crops use bilinear interpolation at one sensor pixel per output pixel to compare a common reference area; interpolation changes noise and star appearance.
  • Overview thumbnails are for navigation. At 1×, each displayed canvas pixel occupies one CSS pixel; 2× duplicates its display size. Your screen’s physical pixel density may differ.
  • The display applies the same percentile limits and asinh stretch to both previews. No photometric matching is applied. Negative values remain in the data; the display maps values below its lower limit to black.
  • Photometric gain records the multiplicative brightness correction, separate from frame weight. Measured means a pixel-based fit (stars or sky blocks), not an independent validation of flux fidelity. Level-only and exposure are fallbacks; search these source names to review them.
  • ISO/gain is the camera header setting, not electrons per ADU. The production noise model is estimated per filter; this audit does not establish optimal weighting across gain settings.
  • A single crop does not cover the whole frame. Check original files and the final master in your astronomy software before accepting a quality decision.
